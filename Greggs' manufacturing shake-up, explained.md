# Greggs' manufacturing shake-up, explained

Nov 25, 2026 · @Abzal

## The short version

Greggs proposes to make its food in fewer places: manufacturing would end at five sites and settle at six. It announced this on 30 September 2026, and says [no final decision has been made](https://corporate.greggs.co.uk/news/Greggs-Q3-Update).

The shops are not part of the plan. Greggs had [2,796 of them](https://assets.greggs.com/f/162306/x/3dfc9eec9a/260930-greggs-q3-trading-update-2026-final.pdf) on 26 September 2026 and is still opening more.

| Question | Answer ([Q3 update, p.3](https://assets.greggs.com/f/162306/x/3dfc9eec9a/260930-greggs-q3-trading-update-2026-final.pdf)) |
| --- | --- |
| Jobs | About 740 roles redundant over two and a half years |
| One-off cash cost | About £60m, of which about £40m is equipment and building spend (capital expenditure) |
| Saving | About £20m a year before tax once complete, realised across 2028 and 2029 |
| Stated reason | Meet capacity needs for growth in the most cost-efficient way |

## How Greggs makes and moves its food

Greggs is *vertically integrated*: it owns factories, warehouses and delivery lorries instead of buying everything from suppliers. It calls this network [a key strength](https://corporate.greggs.co.uk/news/Greggs-Q3-Update) and plans to keep it.

The network has three kinds of site. Some sites do two jobs, making food and delivering it to nearby shops.

- **Manufacturing sites** make products such as savouries, doughnuts, bread rolls and cakes. Each specialises.
- **National distribution centres (NDCs)** hold stock for the whole country: Balliol Park, plus the new Derby and Kettering sites.
- **Radial distribution centres (RDCs)** are regional depots that deliver to shops, for example Amesbury, Birmingham and Enfield.

&#91;embedded content: Greggs supply network · from factory to shop\]

The new NDCs will also [pick individual shop orders by machine](https://a.storyblok.com/f/162306/x/4e33dbb30f/investor-event-supply-plans-may-24.pdf) (slide 23). Regional depots then pass them on, which lets each depot serve more shops.

## The factories, one by one

Five sites would stop manufacturing, three would make less, and three have no specific cut announced. What each made comes from Greggs' [May 2024 network map](https://a.storyblok.com/f/162306/x/4e33dbb30f/investor-event-supply-plans-may-24.pdf) (slide 18). The 2019 volumes come from the [Annual Report 2019](https://www.annualreports.com/HostedData/AnnualReportArchive/g/LSE_GRG_2019.pdf) (PDF p.8) and may be out of date. Proposals come from [Greggs' announcement](https://corporate.greggs.co.uk/news/Greggs-Q3-Update).

| Site | What it makes (2024) | 2019 output | Proposal |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Enfield, north London | Bread rolls, pizza | 40m+ rolls | Factory closes; distribution stays |
| North Lakes, Penrith, Cumbria | Sweet products (biscuits) |  | Closes |
| Pettigrews, Kelso, Scottish Borders | Sandwich relishes, pizza sauce |  | Closes |
| Seaham, County Durham | Not stated |  | Closes |
| Treforest, South Wales | Bread, ring and mini doughnuts |  | Factory ends; distribution stays |
| Clydesmill, Glasgow | Yum Yums, laminated (layered-dough) pastries | 30m+ Yum Yums | Smaller range |
| Manchester | Bread rolls, pizza, pre-mix | 8.5m more pizzas than 2018 | Smaller range |
| Gosforth, Newcastle | Doughnuts, bread | 1.76m doughnuts a week | Tinned bread ends |
| Balliol Park, Newcastle | Savouries (sausage rolls, bakes) | 37m more savouries than 2018 | Stays |

| Derby | New: first line makes sweet products |  | Stays and grows |

Some work will also move between sites, and a few products will be bought from specialist suppliers. Greggs has not said which products go where.

### The sites losing manufacturing

- **North Lakes** opened as a [cake facility around 2009, then a £4.5m purpose-built plant in September 2011](https://cwherald.com/news/greggs-plans-to-close-cumbrian-factory-and-cut-jobs/), supplying about 1,500 shops. It had [about 100 staff in 2011](https://www.thebusinessdesk.com/northwest/news/229779-greggs-4.5m-penrith-site-up-and-running), and gained a [new automated biscuit line](https://assets.greggs.com/f/162306/x/1e7c9ee228/49885-greggs-ar25-interactive-jr-2_reduced.pdf) in 2025 (p.31).
- **Pettigrews** began in 1968 as a [chutney, relish and pickle maker](https://www.scottishdailyexpress.co.uk/news/scottish-news/greggs-close-scottish-factory-despite-37717207) that Greggs later took over. A local councillor [guesses it employs fewer than 100](https://news.stv.tv/scotland/sad-day-for-scottish-workers-as-greggs-plans-to-shut-factory).
- **Treforest** has [about 101 jobs at risk](https://caerphilly.observer/news/1062973/more-than-100-jobs-could-be-at-risk-as-greggs-plans-changes-at-treforest-factory/), according to a local newspaper. Greggs has not confirmed the figure.
- **Enfield** [makes rolls and pizza](https://a.storyblok.com/f/162306/x/4e33dbb30f/investor-event-supply-plans-may-24.pdf). It gained an automated roll plant in 2019. Its distribution centre, opened in 2016, would stay.
- **Seaham** is not on the 2024 map. I found no reliable account of what it makes or when Greggs started there.

**My reading, not Greggs':** both bread sites on the map are affected (Gosforth and Treforest). Bread is therefore a likely candidate for bought-in supply. Rolls and pizza are also squeezed, since Enfield closes and Manchester narrows its range.

## Derby and Kettering: the two new sites

Derby and Kettering are the capacity Greggs is adding while it reduces factories elsewhere. Together with earlier work, they take the logistics network to [about 3,500 shops](https://a.storyblok.com/f/162306/x/fa0ccf33b0/preliminary-results-for-the-52-weeks-ended-28-december-2024.pdf) (2024 results, p.17). Both were under way before the proposal.

|  | Derby | Kettering |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What it does | Frozen manufacturing and distribution | Chilled and ambient (room temperature) distribution |
| Cash budget ([2024 results, p.17](https://a.storyblok.com/f/162306/x/fa0ccf33b0/preliminary-results-for-the-52-weeks-ended-28-december-2024.pdf)) | £135m, including the first production line; building leased for 25 years | £105m, after £30m for the land |
| Timing ([September 2026](https://assets.greggs.com/f/162306/x/3dfc9eec9a/260930-greggs-q3-trading-update-2026-final.pdf)) | Storage, picking and a sweet-product line starting in the last quarter of 2026 | Operational in 2027 |
| Room to grow ([May 2024 plan](https://a.storyblok.com/f/162306/x/4e33dbb30f/investor-event-supply-plans-may-24.pdf), slides 28–32) | Up to five production platforms; two more frozen lines planned, dates open | Space designed for future production and another regional depot |

The [May 2024 plan](https://a.storyblok.com/f/162306/x/4e33dbb30f/investor-event-supply-plans-may-24.pdf) named Yum Yums for Derby's first line, which Clydesmill also makes. Greggs describes Derby as [extra Yum Yum capacity](https://assets.greggs.com/f/162306/x/1e7c9ee228/49885-greggs-ar25-interactive-jr-2_reduced.pdf) (2025 report, p.31). It has not said Yum Yums will leave Glasgow.

The new overheads will [raise costs in 2027](https://assets.greggs.com/f/162306/x/3dfc9eec9a/260930-greggs-q3-trading-update-2026-final.pdf) before the sites start adding to profit.

## Why Greggs is doing it, and the money

Greggs expects more shops and wants to supply them from fewer, better-used factories. It says the changes are needed to meet [capacity requirements for growth in the most cost-efficient way](https://assets.greggs.com/f/162306/x/3dfc9eec9a/260930-greggs-q3-trading-update-2026-final.pdf).

In simple terms, Greggs spends about £60m once to save about £20m every year. The company has not published how the saving splits by site, or between wages and other costs.

### It has done this before

A [£100m programme from 2016](https://a.storyblok.com/f/162306/x/4e33dbb30f/investor-event-supply-plans-may-24.pdf) (slide 11) turned local bakeries that each made many things into specialist factories. It closed Twickenham, Sleaford, Edinburgh and Norwich, and made Birmingham a distribution-only site.

| Product | Sites making it before | Sites after |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Doughnuts | 11 | 1, plus 1 vegan |
| Small cakes | 9 | 1 |
| Bread rolls | 8 | 2 |
| Bread loaves | 8 | 2 |
| Yum Yums | 4 | 1 |

By 2019, yearly net supply cost was [£9.5m below its 2015 level](https://www.annualreports.com/HostedData/AnnualReportArchive/g/LSE_GRG_2019.pdf), against £7.0m expected (Annual Report 2019, PDF p.33). That is encouraging, but it does not prove the new saving will arrive.

## People, reaction and what happens next

The decision is still open, but consultation may now be close to ending. A news report on the day said it would [run until the end of November](https://www.cityam.com/greggs-puts-740-jobs-at-risk-as-baker-braces-for-worse-inflation/). A trade report says it lasts [at least 45 days](https://bakeryinfo.co.uk/manufacture-opinion/greggs-manufacturing-consolidation-called-sensible-for-efficiency-but-union-shares-concerns/724513.article). I found no announced outcome as of 25 November 2026.

- **Staff:** the 740 figure is a total; Greggs has not split it by site. It averaged [3,889 production staff in 2025](https://assets.greggs.com/f/162306/x/1e7c9ee228/49885-greggs-ar25-interactive-jr-2_reduced.pdf) (p.148). Derby is reported to be [hiring about 600](https://bakeryinfo.co.uk/manufacture-opinion/greggs-manufacturing-consolidation-called-sensible-for-efficiency-but-union-shares-concerns/724513.article).
- **Union:** the Bakers, Food and Allied Workers Union says it will [challenge the proposals and seek alternatives](https://foodmanagement.today/greggs-proposes-closure-of-four-uk-manufacturing-sites/) to compulsory redundancies and closures.
- **Politicians:** the Penrith MP is ["extremely disappointed"](https://cwherald.com/news/greggs-plans-to-close-cumbrian-factory-and-cut-jobs/). Members of the Scottish Parliament have [raised concern about Kelso](https://britbrief.co.uk/business/acquisitions/greggs-plans-to-shut-kelso-factory-in-scottish-borders.html).
- **Timing:** changes would start [no earlier than April to June 2027](https://www.lbc.co.uk/article/f2333736a02742d29ed3c3b1b0d04586-5HjdjGD_2/) and run over about two and a half years.

## What is not known

- Costs, output and headcount for each factory.
- Where each moved product would be made, and which would be bought in.
- Whether Treforest is included in the £60m, £20m and 740 figures.
- What happens to the empty buildings, and what they are worth.
- Whether the new Derby spending covers any lines needed for moved products.

## Terms used

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Consultation | Required talks with staff and unions before redundancies are decided |
| Capital expenditure (capex) | Spending on assets that last, such as ovens, production lines and buildings |
| NDC / RDC | National / radial (regional) distribution centre |
| Savoury | Pastry products such as sausage rolls and bakes |
